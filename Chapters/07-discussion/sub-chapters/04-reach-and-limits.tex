\section{Reach and limits of the findings}
\label{discussion:reach-and-limits}

% Academic rewrite
The synthesis closed on the boundary between what the measurements settle and what lies beyond them, and that boundary divides the findings by kind rather than by question. The orderings that follow from format and engine structure are expected to reach beyond the single provider, region, and dataset domain tested, whereas the absolute times and costs attached to them are not (Section~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:threats-to-validity}). Two orderings carry for structural reasons. The per-architecture transfer signature is one: read per architecture in Section~\ref{discussion:single-node-cloud-native-vs-traditional}, and with the larger mover reversing by query pattern (Figure~\ref{fig:single-machine-bytes-transferred}, Figure~\ref{fig:bytes-vs-cardinality}), it is a property of how each format is read rather than a ranking that a change of platform could overturn. Whether distribution repays its coordination cost is the other, set by how large the work must be before added workers amortize a fixed coordination overhead (Figure~\ref{fig:distributed-wall-clock-vs-workers}, Section~\ref{discussion:distribution-and-scaling}). The absolute magnitudes do not generalize in the same way, because they are priced from provider- and region-specific rates whose own predictability varies across providers and over time \parencite{leitner2016_patterns_chaos_iaas,iosup2011_performance_variability_cloud_services,schad2010_runtime_measurements_cloud}. A run-time in seconds or a charge in dollars measured here therefore bounds the comparison to the platform that produced it, while the orderings those numbers express are the part expected to travel.

The operational cost reported throughout is the cost of running a query on a warm engine, not of standing one up to answer a single query. Cluster provisioning and idle time fall outside the timed window by design, so the cost model charges only the interval in which the query executes (Section~\ref{system-architecture-and-implementation:measurement-instrumentation-and-cost-model:what-is-not-measured}). This bounds the cost verdict to amortized, steady workloads, where provisioning is a one-time setup repaid over many queries and the warm-engine figure is the one that recurs. For a sporadic query that pays to provision a cluster it then uses once, the economics shift toward the cold-start and idle charges the timed window excludes, and a configuration that holds a standing cluster, such as the distributed broadcast join on the cost-time frontier (Figure~\ref{fig:distributed-cost-time-pareto}), would carry a setup cost that the steady-state figure does not show \parencite{databricks2024_cost_optimization_best_practices,jonas2019_berkeley_view_on_serverless_computing}. The cost rankings hold as reported for workloads that keep an engine busy; they are a lower bound on the cost of using one intermittently.

The single-node Shapefile findings carry a confound that bounds what they say about the format. The Shapefile path was exercised only through GeoPandas, so a result for that configuration reflects the library and the format together and cannot be attributed to the format in isolation (Section~\ref{discussion:single-node-cloud-native-vs-traditional}, Section~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:statistical-analysis:answering-rq1-cloud-native-vs-traditional}). The clearest instance is the k-nearest-neighbor search, where this path was the slowest configuration measured (Figure~\ref{fig:single-machine-cross-pattern}); that degradation belongs to the GeoPandas and Shapefile stack as run, and a different reader over the same format could place it elsewhere. The finding that this configuration is unsuited to an unindexed nearest-neighbor scan is sound, but it is a statement about the stack, not a verdict on the Shapefile format on its own.

A further bound attaches to the single-node run-time comparison itself: because each engine ran in its native configuration, the comparison varies indexing capability and storage locality together with the engine (Section~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:threats-to-validity}, Section~\ref{system-architecture-and-implementation:compute-configuration}). PostGIS is both the indexed and the co-located configuration, while the GeoParquet path is both unindexed beyond row-group pruning and read across the storage boundary, so the run-time orderings of \acrshort{rq}1 reflect the stacks as they are deployed and cannot be attributed to the engine in isolation. An indexed or co-located GeoParquet configuration was not tested and could narrow the gap; this is a bound on attribution, not a revision of the ordering, which holds as measured for the native deployments compared.

A limitation of statistical power bounds the join comparisons at the smaller tiers, and reading the effect size and the significance test together is what keeps it from looking like a contradiction. Every key pairwise effect size sat at complete separation, with one distribution lying wholly above or below the other (Figure~\ref{fig:vargha-delaney-forest}); yet at the small and medium national-join tiers the run-time and transfer differences carrying those extreme effect sizes were not significant under the Holm adjustment. A large effect size reports that a difference, if real, is total, while the significance test reports whether the sample is large enough to rule out chance, and the two answer different questions \parencite{arcuri2014_hitchhiker_statistical_tests} (Section~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:statistical-analysis:pairwise-comparison-and-effect-size}). The fixed, low iteration count of the join branch leaves the test underpowered, a shortfall the omnibus shares: blocked over only three small-tier cells, the Friedman test carried little power and did not reject the null of equal ranks at the \num{0.05} level (Figure~\ref{fig:pairwise-significance-cliques}), the few-blocks regime in which such an omnibus is known to be weak \parencite{demsar2006_statistical_comparisons_classifiers}. These join entries are therefore read as statistically tied, and the discussion names no run-time winner among them, the extreme effect sizes notwithstanding.

Measuring network transfer at the client boundary extends what the findings can show beyond where a timing-only benchmark stops. The cardinality-independent payload that DuckDB received, near-constant whatever the number of rows a query returned (Figure~\ref{fig:bytes-vs-cardinality}), is the external trace of the range-request read signature established in Section~\ref{discussion:single-node-cloud-native-vs-traditional}, a format-internal behavior that a benchmark recording elapsed time alone leaves invisible. Spatial benchmarks have largely treated network communication as a system-design concern rather than a reported metric, and have not joined format internals to client-observed cost \parencite{tang2020_locationspark_distributed_spatial_query,folkerts2012_benchmarking_in_the_cloud} (Section~\ref{related-work:research-gaps}). Recording the bytes each configuration moved closes that gap for the workloads tested, making visible how each format is read at the boundary where cost accrues. This remains a per-architecture signature rather than a transfer ranking: it locates where each engine performs its work, and it reaches operational cost only through the egress term of the cost model (Equation~\ref{eq:cost-model}), which was zero at the single-node scale tested.

Two of these limits open questions rather than closing them, and the conclusions take them up in full. The small-tier broadcast join approached the PostGIS minimum without overtaking it within the \numrange{2}{16} workers tested, so the worker count at which distribution would overtake a tuned single node on a small join is an extrapolation beyond the tested range rather than a measured crossover (Figure~\ref{fig:distributed-wall-clock-vs-workers}, Section~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:statistical-analysis:answering-rq2-scaling-against-single-node}). The partitioned join, in turn, completed only at the small tier and exhausted executor memory at the medium and large tiers (Figure~\ref{fig:distributed-strategy-contrast}, Table~\ref{tab:distributed-join-descriptive-statistics}), leaving open whether memory tuning would let that strategy complete where it here failed. Both lie at the edge of what was measured, and Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions} returns to them.
