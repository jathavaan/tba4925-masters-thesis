\subsection{Estimator behavior}
\label{appendix:supplementary-results:estimator-illustration}

Figure~\ref{fig:appendix-estimator-illustration} quantifies the minimum-to-mean separation for one representative cell, supporting the estimator choice of Section~\ref{results:overview-and-measurement-quality:timing-distributions-and-estimator-behavior}: the mean (\SI{1.68}{\second}) sat about \num{27}\% above the minimum (\SI{1.33}{\second}), with the median (\SI{1.53}{\second}) between them.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{Chapters/09-appendix/sub-chapters/figures/09-estimator-illustration.pdf}
    \caption[Per-iteration ECDF and estimator choice]{Empirical cumulative distribution
    of the per-iteration elapsed time for one representative cell (point-in-polygon
    lookup, DuckDB over GeoParquet, small tier) on a logarithmic time axis, with the
    minimum, median, and mean marked and the minimum-to-mean span shaded. The
    distribution rises almost vertically off a hard lower bound and then trails across a
    long right tail: the mean (\SI{1.68}{\second}) sits about \num{27}\% above the
    minimum (\SI{1.33}{\second}), with the median (\SI{1.53}{\second}) between them. That
    one-sided separation motivates reporting the minimum rather than the mean
    (Section~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:statistical-analysis:summary-statistics-and-the-choice-of-estimator})
    \parencite{chen2016_robust_benchmarking_noisy_environments}.}
    \label{fig:appendix-estimator-illustration}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Time-of-day stability}
\label{appendix:supplementary-results:time-of-day-stability}

Figure~\ref{fig:appendix-time-of-day-stability} plots each iteration's relative run-time against its start time-of-day: the per-system trend lines were flat (overall Spearman $\rho = -0.01$, and $\lvert\rho\rvert \le 0.16$ within every system), and no time-of-day trend was detectable in the normalized run-times.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{Chapters/09-appendix/sub-chapters/figures/08-time-of-day-stability.pdf}
    \caption[Run-time versus time-of-day]{Per-iteration elapsed time, each normalized to
    the median of its own configuration, query pattern, and size tier cell (relative
    run-time, reference line at \num{1.0}), plotted against the start time-of-day in UTC
    and colored by system family, with a per-system ordinary-least-squares trend line.
    Because the \num{30} passes started at different wall-clock times, each cell's timed
    iterations spread across the clock; the trend lines are flat (overall Spearman
    $\rho = -0.01$, and $\lvert\rho\rvert \le 0.16$ within every system), so no
    time-of-day or noisy-neighbor trend was detectable in the measured run-times.}
    \label{fig:appendix-time-of-day-stability}
\end{figure}

\subsection{CPU-to-wall-clock ratio}
\label{appendix:supplementary-results:cpu-wall-ratio}

Figure~\ref{fig:appendix-cpu-wall-ratio} reports the CPU-to-wall-clock ratio against the one-core reference: DuckDB over GeoParquet recorded a ratio of about \num{3.8}, the local Shapefile path about \num{1.0}, and PostGIS between about \num{0.13} and \num{0.38}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{Chapters/09-appendix/sub-chapters/figures/09-cpu-wall-ratio.pdf}
    \caption[CPU-to-wall-clock ratio]{Ratio of CPU time to wall-clock time,
    $(\mathrm{cpu}_{\mathrm{user}} + \mathrm{cpu}_{\mathrm{sys}})/\mathrm{elapsed}$, for
    each single-machine configuration across the query patterns and size tiers, with a
    reference line at \num{1.0} (one fully-busy core). The ratio is computed from
    per-cell sums of CPU and wall-clock time, to be robust to the sub-millisecond
    resolution of the per-iteration CPU counters. Values above \num{1.0} correspond to
    more than one core busy on average, and values below \num{1.0} to less than one.
    Color encodes configuration; the source counter is
    \texttt{psutil.Process.cpu\_times}.}
    \label{fig:appendix-cpu-wall-ratio}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Same-engine size scaling}
\label{appendix:supplementary-results:size-scaling}

Figure~\ref{fig:appendix-size-scaling-slope} traces each engine's wall-clock minimum from the small to the large tier: DuckDB over GeoParquet grew by roughly \num{21} to \num{22} times across the three patterns (a factor of \num{22.5} for the point-in-polygon lookup, \num{21.6} for the bounding-box filter, and \num{21.1} for the k-nearest-neighbor search), whereas PostGIS stayed nearly flat (between about \num{0.9} and \num{1.4} times, and slightly faster at the large tier for the point-in-polygon and k-nearest-neighbor patterns); the local Shapefile path ran the small tier only.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Chapters/09-appendix/sub-chapters/figures/09-size-scaling-slope.pdf}
    \caption[Same-engine size scaling]{Wall-clock minimum of each single-machine engine
    across the size tiers, one panel per query pattern, on a logarithmic axis with the
    \num{95}\% bootstrap confidence interval. Each multi-tier line is annotated with its
    growth factor (the largest tier divided by the smallest tier). The Shapefile
    configuration ran at the small tier only and is shown as a single small-tier point
