\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{introduction:scope-and-limitations}
Vector workloads on Norwegian building polygons derived from conflated \acrfull{osm} and \acrfull{fkb}\footnote{\acrshort{fkb} is the most detailed vector data available, managed by the Norwegian Mapping Authority.} data bound the benchmark, leaving raster data and other feature types outside it. Four configurations are compared: DuckDB over GeoParquet, PostGIS, GeoPandas over Shapefile, and Apache Sedona on Databricks. The comparison addresses read performance alone, setting ingestion, update, and write performance aside. It exercises four workloads: point-in-polygon, k-nearest-neighbor, bounding-box, and spatial-join, each evaluated on run-time, network transfer, and operational cost across small, medium, and large dataset tiers. The single-machine patterns run at the small and large tiers, the join across all three.

Confining the comparison to one controlled setting imposes several limits. The first is its reliance on a single cloud provider, Microsoft Azure. Operational cost reflects a pricing snapshot and shifts as vendor rates change. Queries are issued by a single client in sequence, so multi-user concurrency and contention go unmeasured. Distributed scaling is observed only over 2 to 16 workers, and no traditional format is tested in a distributed setting, since none targets one. These limitations bound how far the findings generalize. Their effect on the validity of the measurements is treated among the threats to validity in Section~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:threats-to-validity}.
