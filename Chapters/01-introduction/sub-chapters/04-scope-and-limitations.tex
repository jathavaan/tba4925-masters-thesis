\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{introduction:scope-and-limitations}
This study is bounded to vector workloads on Norwegian building polygons derived from conflated \acrfull{osm} and \acrfull{fkb} data. Raster data and other feature types lie outside it. The comparison covers four configurations, DuckDB over GeoParquet, PostGIS, GeoPandas over Shapefile, and Apache Sedona on Databricks, and addresses read performance alone, leaving ingestion, update, and write performance out of scope. It exercises the point-in-polygon, k-nearest-neighbor, bounding-box, and spatial-join workloads. Each workload is evaluated on run-time, network transfer, and operational cost across small, medium, and large dataset tiers, with the single-machine patterns run at the small and large tiers and the join across all three.

Holding the comparison to one controlled setting imposes several boundaries. The benchmark runs on a single cloud provider, Microsoft Azure. Operational cost reflects a pricing snapshot and shifts as vendor rates change. Queries are issued by a single client in sequence, leaving multi-user concurrency and contention unmeasured. Distributed scaling is observed only over 2 to 16 workers, and no traditional format is tested in a distributed setting, since none targets one. These limitations bound how far the findings generalize. Their effect on the validity of the measurements is treated among the threats to validity in Chapter~\ref{research-design-and-methodology:threats-to-validity}.
