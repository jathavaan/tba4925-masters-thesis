\section{Problem statement and objectives}
\label{introduction:problem-statement-and-objectives}
This thesis evaluates how cloud-native geospatial technologies compare against traditional ones for vector workloads, along three dimensions: read performance, network transfer, and operational cost. The comparison spans two settings, single-machine queries over one dataset and a national-scale spatial join in which a distributed engine is set against single-node engines on the same data.

These objectives are framed as three research questions. Because they follow from gaps in the existing benchmarking literature, the research questions are stated formally in Section~\ref{related-work:research-questions}, after the related work that motivates them in Chapter~\ref{ch:related-work}. In outline, the first compares the cloud-native combination of GeoParquet and DuckDB against the traditional alternatives PostGIS and Shapefile; the second extends the comparison to the horizontal scaling of Apache Sedona against single-node engines on a national-scale spatial join; and the third asks whether the best-performing configuration is consistent across query patterns and outcome dimensions.
