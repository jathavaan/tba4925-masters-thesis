\section{Contributions}
\label{introduction:contributions}
% Academic rewrite
Running on identical Norwegian building data in the same cloud storage, the benchmark places vector cloud-native formats and engines beside their traditional counterparts, addressing gaps that prior spatial benchmarks leave open. Four contributions follow. The first, \acrshort{rq}1, compares the cloud-native combination of GeoParquet and DuckDB against PostGIS and Shapefile across spatial intersection, nearest-neighbor search, and bounding-box filtering. The second, \acrshort{rq}2, sets the distributed engine Apache Sedona against single-node PostGIS and DuckDB on a national-scale spatial join across small, medium, and large dataset tiers, a comparison no published benchmark has reported. It locates the scale at which a single node ceases to be feasible: at the large tier neither single-node engine completes the join while the distributed broadcast join does, so distribution there is a question of feasibility rather than speed. The third reports network transfer jointly with run-time and operational cost, a dimension absent from almost all spatial benchmarks. The fourth is methodological: it reports effect sizes, the magnitude of a difference rather than only its presence, and bootstrapped \acrlonglc{ci}s~(\acrshort{ci}) and adopts a rank-based omnibus test, one overall test of whether the configurations differ, with post-hoc correction, practices rarely found in geospatial benchmarking.
